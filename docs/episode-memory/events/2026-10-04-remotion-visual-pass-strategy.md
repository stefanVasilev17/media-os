# EP001 Visual Strategy Update — Remotion

Status: ACTIVE DIRECTION

## Production technology
The visual production strategy is Remotion-first. Do not plan EP001 visual execution around Spline. Existing Spline-era ideas may be reused only as conceptual references, but choreography, camera, component states, timing, reuse, and rendering should be designed for Remotion.

## Canonical visual-pass workflow
1. Episode-level visual pass: define how the entire ~20 minute film breathes, including close/medium/wide/full-map rhythm, architecture growth, Phone returns, mini-world reveals, failure-branch separation, tension and release, and major transitions.
2. Shot-level visual pass: lock exact choreography for SHOT 01 through SHOT 16 inside the episode-level direction.
3. Component consistency pass: unify recurring concepts such as Request, Lookup, Decision, Waiting, Failure, Response, Proof, and Restore into one reusable visual grammar.

## Creative goal
The viewer should feel they are watching one continuous story, not reading a technical textbook or watching 16 disconnected diagrams. Technical components must be introduced carefully, only when the story creates a reason to look at them. The visual design should maximize retention by maintaining narrative momentum, curiosity, causal clarity, and meaningful visual change without decorative motion.

## Retention rule
Use meaningful visual change rather than constant camera movement. Roughly every 5–10 seconds, at least one meaningful state should evolve: active object, depth, information layer, request position, causal relationship, system state, or human consequence. Major perspective changes must remain rare enough to feel earned.

## Remotion implications
Design the episode as a reusable component system with deterministic timing and frame-accurate semantic anchors. Prefer one continuous architecture world with reusable React/Remotion components, camera transforms, local mini-world reveals, state transitions, and reusable concept primitives rather than isolated one-off scenes.
